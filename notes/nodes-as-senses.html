Nodes as Senses

Should a knowledge graph node be a string, or a region of embedding space? The literature says yes-but. Four measurements on your own corpus say: not here, not yet, and the thing you'd actually fix first is a two-line NER filter.

The proposal, taken apart

Four claims wearing one coat

"Use embeddings as nodes" bundles four separable proposals. They have wildly different evidence behind them, different implementation costs, and different payoffs on the datasets you're running. Splitting them up is most of the analysis.

S Split One surface form, many nodes — Mercury the planet is not Mercury the element. Nothing to bite
M Merge Many surface forms, one node — Obama, Barack Obama, the 44th president. Solved-ish
L Language-universal The same node for dog, chien, собака, because the encoder already colocates them. Sound, off-thesis
V No symbols at all The node is a vector; adjacency is nearest-neighbour search at query time. Tried, stalled

Verdicts are argued below, and four of them are argued from measurements on this repo's data rather than from priors. The short version: M is worth about six points of chain connectivity beyond a trivial normaliser; S is the half the field is excited about and the half MuSiQue cannot support, because true lexical polysemy barely occurs there; L is the most intellectually satisfying and the least testable in your current harness; V is a road already walked in 2020–2022. The measurements are the contribution here — the negative ones especially.

Prior art

Yes, and some of it is a decade old

The radical version already exists — it's called a virtual knowledge base

DrKIT (Dhingra et al., ICLR 2020) is claim V executed in full. Every entity mention in the corpus gets a contextual embedding; there are no canonical entity nodes; multi-hop traversal is a sequence of sparse-matrix products interleaved with maximum inner-product search over the mention index. It "softly follows paths of relations between mentions," reports +9 points on 3-hop MetaQA, and runs 10–100× faster than iterative retrievers.

The thing worth staring at: DrKIT's traversal operator and LinearRAG's are the same shape. Sparse incidence matrix out, dense similarity in the middle, sparse matrix back. Your σ_max gate lives in exactly the slot where DrKIT puts its MIPS call. This is not a coincidence — it's the only efficient way to do soft graph propagation, and both papers found it independently.

Entities as Experts (Févry et al., EMNLP 2020) and Mention Memory / TOME (de Jong et al., ICLR 2022) push it further: TOME holds a table of 150M Wikipedia mention encodings and lets transformer layers attend into it. Nodes as embeddings, at scale, six years ago.

So why isn't everyone doing this? Because all three need end-to-end training of the mention encoder — DrKIT's paper spends significant effort on a pretraining scheme with KB-derived hard negatives — and that conflicts with the drop-in, no-training posture that made RAG win. The line didn't fail on the merits; it got out-competed by "just embed the passage." That's an opening, not a tombstone.

The merge half is standard practice, done cheaply

HippoRAG already implements claim M — but as edges rather than identity. Phrase nodes whose embeddings exceed cosine τ = 0.8 get an extra synonymy edge; PageRank then flows across it. This is a deliberate hedge: a wrong edge dilutes, a wrong merge is unrecoverable.

DEG-RAG (Oct 2025) does the hard version and is the best current evidence for it — the first systematic sweep of blocking strategies, embedding choices, similarity metrics and merge operators for LLM-built KGs. Result: ~40% smaller graph, consistently better QA across several graph-RAG backbones. CESI (WWW 2018) is the pre-LLM ancestor, clustering noun-phrase embeddings with side information to canonicalise open KBs.

The split half is where the field is currently pointing

This is the live one. TIGRAG (June 2026) builds token co-occurrence graphs — nodes are bare tokens, no NER, no LLM extraction — and beats LightRAG, HippoRAG2 and NaiveRAG on HotpotQA / 2Wiki / MuSiQue. Its conclusion names your idea as the next step, verbatim:

"we plan to address the inherent limitations of treating tokens as only lexical units, by introducing semantic-aware nodes to address token polysemy."

SPRIG arrives at the same place from the other direction: it builds a CPU-only bipartite entity–document graph, admits it does no entity linking or coreference, and patches around ambiguity with a title-alias map, IDF weighting and df(e)^-p hub downweighting. Those patches are the tell — the surface form is doing a job it isn't good enough for.

NodeRAG is the closest thing to a counter-argument: it also concluded that one node type per entity is wrong, but solved it by typing nodes (entities, semantic units, attributes, community summaries) rather than by contextualising them. Two roads out of the same complaint.

Word sense induction is the thirty-year-old version of "split"

Clustering contextual embeddings of a word to recover its senses is a mature subfield with a discouraging verdict — a 2026 survey is titled, flatly, "In the LLM era, Word Sense Induction remains unsolved." Two findings from it matter for your design:

  • Word identity dominates sense. In contextual embedding space, all occurrences of a token cluster tightly together and sense structure is a faint sub-signal inside that cluster. Naive clustering over all mentions recovers the vocabulary, not the senses. Corollary: you must block by surface form and cluster within the block.
  • Anisotropy contaminates the metric. The top principal components of contextual space encode frequency and position, not meaning. Centre and whiten per-block before you measure any distance.

And the classical warning, which you should take seriously: Sanderson (SIGIR 1994) showed that sense disambiguation only helps retrieval above ~90% accuracy, and that 20–30% error is enough to wipe out the gain entirely. Ambiguity mostly hurts short queries; long ones disambiguate themselves. Your sub-questions are short. That cuts both ways — it means the gate is exposed to ambiguity, and it means a sloppy splitter will hurt you.

The language-universal claim is better supported than the synonym claim

This is the non-obvious one. Embedding cosine measures distributional relatedness, not referential identity. Within a language that's a serious problem: Obama and Bush sit closer together than Obama and the 44th president, so any threshold loose enough to catch the paraphrase is loose enough to merge the siblings. Across languages the geometry is kinder — translation equivalence is what multilingual encoders like LaBSE are explicitly trained to retrieve, so cross-lingual merging is a better-posed problem than monolingual synonym merging. LMMS, SensEmBERT and ARES all exploit exactly this to build multilingual sense inventories over BabelNet synsets; MTransE and Prix-LM are the KG-side lineage.

Measurement

What your corpus actually says

Rather than argue from priors, I measured both halves on data/musique.json. LinearRAG keys an entity node on md5 of the exact spaCy span, so two sentences are adjacent only if they contain a byte-identical entity string. That makes both questions concrete.

The merge half: how often is the bridge join broken by surface form?

For every gold chain step where sub-question t+1 references answer #t, I checked whether that bridge string is lexically present in both the producing and the consuming paragraph.

So the ceiling on claim M is about 11 points of chain connectivity, of which the cheap normaliser gets nearly half. A lowercase-and-strip pass — three lines in ner.py — captures 4.9%. Everything an embedding-based merger could add beyond that tops out around 6.3%, and it has to earn that without over-merging. This is a real but modest prize, and it explains why HippoRAG's τ=0.8 synonymy edge was good enough for HippoRAG.

That looks like a mandate for splitting. It is not, and the next two measurements are why.

Why the fan-out exists — and it isn't polysemy

Fan-out has three possible causes, and they call for three different fixes: genuine polysemy (wants sense splitting), one referent discussed everywhere (wants IDF weighting), and spans that should never have been nodes (wants a type filter). Classifying the 101+ band settles it:

Is there any polysemy to split?

Wikipedia hands you free referent labels: a title with a parenthetical disambiguator is an explicit statement that this surface form denotes more than one thing. Counting heads that map to multiple distinct articles:

And most of MuSiQue's 54 are not homonyms at all — they are facets of one work: American Idol (season 1) vs (season 16), Back to the Future vs (franchise) vs (soundtrack). Genuine homonymy is a handful of cases (Australia the country / the film / the continent). 2Wiki is materially better — four distinct Picture of Dorian Gray films, three different Archduchesses Maria Theresa separated only by date ranges.

Fan-out is real; polysemy is not what causes it. Two thirds of the worst band is NER type junk, and the remainder is overwhelmingly one referent discussed in many paragraphs — the case where splitting is actively wrong.

So the honest ordering on this data is the reverse of where the field's excitement points. The cheap fix — dropping DATE, NORP, TIME, PERCENT, MONEY, QUANTITY from the node set, two lines in ner.py — removes 63.6% of the worst hub mass. What remains is legitimate high-frequency entities, and the textbook treatment for those is IDF weighting or df(e)^-p downweighting, which is exactly what SPRIG already does. Sense modelling is the third thing to reach for, not the first.

Reframing

Don't split the node. Make it route.

The measurements above rule out hard partitioning, and they also rule out the obvious soft version. Consider the two cases the kernel must separate, both of which have low topical similarity:

  • Maradona@Barcelona ↔ Maradona@Napoli — same referent, must stay connected.
  • Mercury@planet ↔ Mercury@record-label — different referent, must be severed.

No sentence-context embedding separates those, because both pairs are topically distant. Worse, weighting the transfer by contextual similarity would suppress the first case — and the first case is the multi-hop chain. A bridge sentence and its answer sentence are dissimilar by construction; that is the premise of the σ_max work. A naively contextual kernel would attenuate exactly the hops the gate exists to enable.

Two attempts to find an intrinsic signal that separates them failed on this corpus. Schütze-style second-order context vectors — the founding word-sense-induction method, an IDF-weighted bag of the co-occurring entities — produced a separation statistic with median 0.998, saturated and therefore meaningless: exact-span neighbourhoods are too sparse for any pair of paragraphs to overlap, so every 2-way split looks perfect. And the silver labels needed to grade a better attempt don't exist at scale, per the disambiguator count above.

So the way out is not a better mᵢ. It is to stop trying to freeze meaning at index time. Meaning depends on context — which means the corpus cannot answer which Mercury on its own, and does not have to. At retrieval time the question already says which one. Keep the entity node whole, and make the transfer through it conditional on the active query set: the node stops broadcasting and starts routing, with the routing weight supplied by the sub-questions you already generate.

This also settles a design question the literature keeps stumbling over. If you make nodes into embeddings and edges into nearest-neighbour similarity, you have reinvented dense retrieval with extra steps — the whole value of a graph is that adjacency encodes co-occurrence, which is non-metric and not recoverable from the embedding space. So: keep edges structural, make only the transfer semantic.

The change

What this looks like in LinearRAG

Today the propagation is, with a over entities, M ∈ {0,1}|S|×|E|, and a single collapsed gate:

σ_max = MAX_j sim(q_j, ·)                    # collapse the query set FIRST
at    = MAX( Mᵀ ( σ_max ⊙ (M at-1) ), at-1 )   # then propagate once

That collapse is where the fan-out damage happens, and it is unrelated to node identity. Once σ_max is a single vector, every sub-question's relevance is pooled into one conductance map — so a hub entity conducts activation opened by q₁ into sentences that were only ever relevant to q₃. The sub-questions cross-contaminate each other's paths. Reversing the order fixes it:

# one propagation per sub-question; collapse at the END, not the start
for j in range(m):
    σ_j = sim(q_j, ·)
    a_j = MAX( Mᵀ ( σ_j ⊙ (M a_j) ), a_j )   # independent branch
score = MAX_j a_j                          # max over branches, over entities

This is the whole change, and it needs no mention vectors, no sense inventory, no thresholds, and no new signal of any kind. It gets the routing behaviour the diagram shows — a hub still conducts, but only within one sub-question's branch, so Mercury-the-planet activation cannot arrive at a record-label sentence unless some sub-question is actually about record labels.

  • It keeps your floor argument. q₀ is the original question, so branch 0 is vanilla LinearRAG, and the final max is over a set containing it. Still cannot score below vanilla by construction — the same property gate.py is built around.
  • It is strictly more expressive than the current σ_max. Pooling the gate first is a lossy projection of this; you can recover the current arm by pooling, so one binary serves both.
  • Cost is m× propagation, not m× encoding. The embeddings, the graph, and M are shared; only the sparse products repeat, and they batch — the m branches are independent columns of one matrix product. On MuSiQue m is typically 2–4.

What this does not need

Notably absent: the mention layer, V Vᵀ, and every clustering decision. The measurements are what removed them. If you want the sense-aware version later, it slots into the same operator as a per-branch mask, and the right corpus to try it on is 2Wiki, not MuSiQue.

And the merge half, if you want it

Merging stays orthogonal and cheap: union-find over surface forms, with candidates from containment and acronym rules rather than raw cosine. The measurement says the semantic residue beyond normalisation is 6.3%, and a cosine threshold loose enough to catch it will merge siblings — Obama and Bush sit closer in embedding space than Obama and the 44th president. Require corroboration if you do it at all: two forms merge only if they already share a graph neighbour.

Experiment

How to find out cheaply

The diagnostics are done and they redirected the work, which is what diagnostics are for. What remains is four arms on the existing matrix, ordered cheapest-first, all reusing dataio, metrics and the current gate patch.

G0
Vanilla
Surface hash, pooled σ_q. The floor.
G1
Type filter
Drop DATE / NORP / TIME / PERCENT / MONEY / QUANTITY. Two lines; removes 63.6% of hub mass.
G2
Normalise + alias
lower + strip punct, then containment union-find. Caps at ~11%.
G3
Per-branch propagation
One pass per sub-question, max at the end. The proposal.

Cross G1–G3 with σ_q and σ_max. The prediction worth committing to in advance: G1 and G3 attack the same measured problem — hub fan-out — by different routes, so they should not be additive. If G1 alone captures most of G3's margin, the honest conclusion is that LinearRAG's hub problem is a NER configuration bug and the operator change is unnecessary. That is a perfectly good result and it belongs in the paper either way.

Two diagnostics to log alongside recall@k, because they explain why rather than whether: bridge-join rate (fraction of gold chains whose bridge lands in a shared node — 88.9% at baseline, so there is only 11 points of room and you should see it saturate) and activation purity (share of propagated mass reaching gold vs. distractor passages, per hop). Purity is where G1 and G3 should move first, and it will move well before recall@k does.

One environment note: every measurement in this memo used a capitalised-span regex as a stand-in for spaCy, because spaCy is not installed in the mbuzai env. The fan-out and bridge-join numbers are robust to that — they concern string identity, not span boundaries — but the arms above need faithful NER to reproduce LinearRAG's node set, so spacy + en_core_web_sm should go into the mbuzai env before G1 is run.

Assessment

Is it promising?

As a research direction in general: yes. As something to build on this corpus for this paper: no, and the measurements are unusually clear about it.

The idea is sound and the field agrees — TIGRAG names it as their next step, SPRIG's limitations section circles it, and the virtual-KB line built it three times. But every route into it on MuSiQue is blocked by the same finding: the conditions the idea needs are not present in the data. Merging has ~6% of headroom past a trivial normaliser. Splitting needs polysemy, and 54 ambiguous heads out of 9,838 titles — most of them season numbers rather than homonyms — is not enough to move a retrieval metric. The fan-out that looked like a mandate for sense modelling turned out to be 63.6% dates and nationalities.

Three of the four diagnostics came back negative, and that is worth more than the memo I would have written if they hadn't. It converts "here is a promising direction" into "here is a two-line fix that captures most of the available gain, and here is why the sophisticated version has nothing to work with." Both halves are publishable; the second half is the kind of negative result the graph-RAG literature is conspicuously missing, since TIGRAG and SPRIG are both proposing to walk into it.

What survives intact is the reformulation: don't freeze meaning at index time, because you can't — resolve it at query time, where the question already disambiguates. That drops out as per-branch propagation, which needs no sense inventory at all and is a direct extension of the σ_max thesis rather than a second axis bolted onto it.

The language-universal property is real, follows for free from a multilingual encoder, and is better-posed than monolingual synonymy — translation equivalence is what LaBSE-class encoders are trained to retrieve, whereas cosine within a language conflates relatedness with identity. But MuSiQue and 2Wiki are monolingual and no established multi-hop retrieval benchmark would show the gain. It is a separate project with its own dataset construction, and that is a much larger undertaking than it looks.

If you want to pursue the split half anyway

  • Change corpus. 2Wiki has 89 ambiguous heads over 202 articles with genuine homonymy — four Picture of Dorian Gray films, three Archduchesses Maria Theresa separated only by date ranges. Its comparison and bridge-comparison question types are also where entity confusion should actually bite.
  • Get real NER first. Everything sense-level in this memo is untrustworthy until spaCy replaces the regex proxy; the proxy emitted Listed, Pick and Michael J as entities.
  • Respect Sanderson's bar. Disambiguation below ~90% accuracy has never paid off in retrieval, and word sense induction has spent thirty years not clearing it — a 2026 survey says so in its title.
